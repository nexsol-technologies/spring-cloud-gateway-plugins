The calls of the service graph, laid out as they run: the callers that reached the gateway on the left, the services it reached on the right.
A dot travels a hop when calls arrive on it, coloured by how those calls came back: it is the traffic since the last poll, not a replay of one request. A quiet hop stays still, which is the point — the picture only moves when the gateway is carrying something. Turn Auto off to stop polling.
The line of a hop is its last traffic: it turns amber or red when calls come back 4xx or 5xx, and back to green as soon as a batch comes back clean. The dot resting at its end is everything it has carried and only ever gets worse, so a hop that has misbehaved stays recognisable once its line has gone green again.
A service that also calls appears on both sides, once as what it reached and once as what reached through the gateway. The graph view merges the two into one node; here they are two ends of a flow, which is what the two columns are for.
